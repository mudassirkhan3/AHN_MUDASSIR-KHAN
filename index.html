01. Tracheostomy Care & Tracheal Suctioning

Station 01 & 02

📌 1. Definition

Tracheostomy care involves cleansing the stoma, replacing dressings, and suctioning secretions from the artificial airway to maintain patency and prevent pulmonary infections.

🎯 2. Purpose

  • Maintain airway patency and oxygenation.
  • Prevent stoma infection and skin breakdown.
  • Remove accumulated pulmonary secretions.

⚠️ 3. Key Precautions / Risks

Avoid aggressive suctioning in severe bronchospasm or uncorrected hypoxia. Never suction for longer than 10–15 seconds per pass.

🛠️ 4. Required Equipment

Sterile suction catheter kit, suction machine, sterile normal saline, hydrogen peroxide (if cleaning inner cannula), sterile gloves, tracheostomy ties, pre-cut split gauze.

📋 5. Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Preparation: Verify physician order, explain procedure, and perform hand hygiene.
  2. Pre-Oxygenation: Hyperoxygenate the patient with 100% O₂ for 30–60 seconds.
  3. Sterile Suctioning: Don sterile gloves. Gently insert catheter without applying suction until resistance is met or patient coughs. Apply intermittent suction while withdrawing and rotating catheter (≤ 10–15s).
  4. Stoma Care: Clean around the stoma using sterile normal saline with single outward wipes.
  5. Securement: Replace the tracheostomy ties (keep one finger space under ties) and apply fresh split-drain dressing.
✅ Conclusion & Nursing Alert: Re-assess breath sounds and SpO₂ immediately after the procedure. Document secretion color, consistency, and stoma site appearance.

02. Assisting in Lumbar Puncture (LP)

Station 03 & 09

📌 1. Definition

Insertion of a spinal needle into the subarachnoid space (typically at L3-L4 or L4-L5 interspace) to measure pressure or collect Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF).

🎯 2. Purpose

  • Diagnose meningitis, encephalitis, or subarachnoid hemorrhage.
  • Measure CSF pressure and administer spinal anesthesia or intrathecal meds.

⚠️ 3. Contraindications

Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP) (risk of brain herniation), skin infection over the puncture site, or severe bleeding disorders.

🛠️ 4. Required Equipment

Sterile LP tray, spinal needle with stylet (20–22 gauge), manometer, sterile collection tubes (numbered 1–3/4), local anesthetic (Lidocaine), antiseptics.

📋 5. Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Positioning (Crucial): Position patient laterally in a fetal position (chin to chest, knees pulled to abdomen) or sitting bent forward over a table.
  2. Sterile Prep: Assist provider with skin disinfection and sterile field setup.
  3. Specimen Collection: Support and maintain patient position while the physician collects 3–4 CSF specimen vials.
  4. Post-Procedure Care: Place a sterile dressing over the site and instruct the patient to lie flat for 4–8 hours to prevent spinal headache.
✅ Conclusion & Nursing Alert: Label CSF tubes sequentially at the bedside. Monitor the puncture site for CSF leakage or hematoma and perform frequent neuro checks.

03. Skin Traction & Cast Immobilization

Station 14 & 15

📌 1. Definition

Application of pulling force (Buck’s traction) directly to skin/soft tissues, or applying plaster/fiberglass cast to immobilize fractured bones.

🎯 2. Purpose

Reduce muscle spasms, maintain proper bone alignment, realign fractures, and provide joint stability pre- or post-surgery.

⚠️ 3. Contraindications

Severe skin lacerations/infections under traction site, allergy to adhesive, active compartment syndrome, or compromised circulation.

🛠️ 4. Required Equipment

Traction boot/straps, pulley set, prescribed weights (usually 5–10 lbs max for skin traction), stockinette, padding, plaster/fiberglass rolls, warm water basin.

📋 5. Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Assessment: Assess skin integrity and perform baseline neurovascular check (5 Ps: Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis).
  2. Traction Setup: Wrap limb securely with boot/straps. Attach rope over pulley system and apply prescribed weights gently.
  3. Weight Management: Ensure weights hang **completely free** without resting on floor or bed frame.
  4. Cast Care: If applying plaster, handle wet cast with **palms of hands only** (never fingertips) to prevent pressure points.
✅ Conclusion & Nursing Alert: Perform neurovascular checks every 1–2 hours initially. Report sudden increasing pain or loss of pulse immediately to rule out Compartment Syndrome.

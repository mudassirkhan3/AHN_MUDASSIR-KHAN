Stations 01 & 02

Tracheostomy Care & Suctioning

📌 Definition

  • Cleansing the stoma and replacing inner cannula.
  • Removing secretions from the artificial airway.
  • Maintaining sterile patency to prevent infection.

🎯 Purpose

  • Maintain a clear airway and improve oxygenation.
  • Prevent stoma tissue breakdown and infection.
  • Facilitate the removal of thick pulmonary secretions.

⚠️ Contraindications / Precautions

  • Severe uncorrected hypoxia or active bronchospasm.
  • Max Suction Time: Never exceed 10–15 seconds per pass.
  • Avoid aggressive suctioning in bleeding disorders.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Sterile suction catheter kit & suction machine.
  • Sterile normal saline & Hydrogen peroxide.
  • Sterile gloves, pre-cut split gauze, & fresh trach ties.

Reassess SpO₂ and breath sounds continuously during and after suctioning.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

0 / 5
  1. Preparation: Perform hand hygiene, verify orders, and explain the procedure to the patient.
  2. Pre-Oxygenation: Hyperoxygenate the patient using 100% O₂ for 30–60 seconds prior to suctioning.
  3. Sterile Suctioning: Don sterile gloves. Insert catheter gently without suction. Apply intermittent suction while withdrawing and rotating (max 15 sec).
  4. Stoma Care: Cleanse stoma area with sterile saline using single outward wiping motions.
  5. Securement: Apply new split-drain dressing and secure ties (allow one finger space beneath ties).
✅

Conclusion & Viva Point: Always re-assess breath sounds and SpO₂ immediately after. Document the amount, color, and consistency of secretions.

Stations 03 & 09

Assisting in Lumbar Puncture (LP)

📌 Definition

  • Insertion of a spinal needle into the subarachnoid space.
  • Usually performed at the L3-L4 or L4-L5 vertebral interspace.

🎯 Purpose

  • Collect Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) for diagnostic testing (e.g., Meningitis).
  • Measure intracranial fluid pressure.
  • Administer intrathecal medications or spinal anesthesia.

⚠️ Contraindications

  • Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP) (risk of fatal brain herniation).
  • Skin infections over the lumbar puncture site.
  • Severe coagulopathies or bleeding disorders.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Sterile LP tray, manometer, & local anesthetic (Lidocaine).
  • Spinal needle with stylet (20–22 gauge).
  • 3 to 4 sequential sterile specimen collection tubes.

Fetal / lateral position — chin tucked, knees drawn to abdomen — opens the vertebral interspace.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

0 / 4
  1. Positioning (Crucial Step): Place patient in a lateral fetal position or sitting bent forward to open vertebral spaces.
  2. Site Prep: Assist the physician with sterile skin disinfection using Betadine/Chlorhexidine.
  3. Collection: Maintain the patient's position strictly while the provider measures pressure and collects CSF into sequentially numbered tubes.
  4. Post-Care: Apply sterile dressing. Instruct the patient to remain lying flat for 4–8 hours to prevent spinal fluid leaks and headaches.
✅

Conclusion & Viva Point: Always label CSF tubes at the bedside. Monitor the puncture site for hematoma and perform frequent neurological checks post-procedure.

Stations 14 & 15

Skin Traction & Cast Immobilization

📌 Definition

  • Traction: Application of a pulling force to skin/soft tissues (e.g., Buck's traction).
  • Cast: Rigid dressing (plaster/fiberglass) applied to immobilize fractured bones.

🎯 Purpose

  • Relieve severe muscle spasms and pain pre-surgery.
  • Realign bone fragments and maintain anatomical position.
  • Provide stability to injured joints during healing.

⚠️ Contraindications

  • Severe skin abrasions or infections under the traction site.
  • Active Compartment Syndrome (requires immediate removal).
  • Compromised arterial circulation to the limb.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Traction boots, ropes, and pulley frames.
  • Prescribed weights (typically 5–10 lbs for skin traction).
  • Stockinette padding, fiberglass rolls, & warm water.

Weights must hang freely — never resting on the bed or floor — to maintain constant pulling force.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

0 / 4
  1. Assessment: Perform a baseline neurovascular assessment checking the 5 P's (Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis).
  2. Traction Application: Wrap the limb in the traction boot. Attach the rope over the pulley system and apply prescribed weights gently.
  3. Weight Verification: Ensure weights hang completely free and do not rest on the bed or floor.
  4. Cast Handling: If assisting with a wet plaster cast, handle it using the palms of the hands only (never fingertips) to prevent pressure ulcers.
✅

Conclusion & Viva Point: Continuous neurovascular monitoring is critical. Report unrelenting pain that is out of proportion to the injury immediately to rule out Compartment Syndrome.

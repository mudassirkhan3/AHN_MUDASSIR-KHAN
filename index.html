Station 01

1. Tracheostomy Care

📌 Definition

  • Cleaning the stoma and inner cannula of a tracheostomy tube.
  • Replacing dressings and ties under aseptic technique.

🎯 Purpose

  • Maintain airway patency and tissue integrity.
  • Prevent stomal infection and skin breakdown.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Always keep a spare trach tube at the bedside.
  • Hold outer cannula securely while changing ties.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Trach care kit, sterile saline, H₂O₂.
  • Sterile gloves, pre-cut split gauze, trach ties.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Perform hand hygiene, don PPE, and position patient in semi-Fowler’s position.
  2. Unlock and remove inner cannula; soak and clean with sterile saline.
  3. Clean around the stoma using saline-soaked swabs with single outward strokes.
  4. Replace pre-cut split drain dressing and secure ties allowing 1 finger space.
✅ Viva Point: Never cut standard gauze for a trach stoma—frayed threads can be aspirated. Always use pre-cut split drain sponges.
Station 02

2. Tracheal Suctioning

📌 Definition

  • Mechanical aspiration of pulmonary secretions via an artificial airway.

🎯 Purpose

  • Clear airway secretions, improve ventilation, and collect sputum samples.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Max Time: Never suction longer than 10–15 seconds per pass.
  • Avoid applying suction during catheter insertion.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Sterile suction catheter kit, suction unit, sterile gloves, normal saline.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Hyperoxygenate the patient with 100% O₂ for 30–60 seconds.
  2. Don sterile gloves; insert catheter gently without suction until resistance is met.
  3. Apply intermittent suction while rotating and withdrawing the catheter (≤10–15s).
  4. Reoxygenate patient and reassess lung sounds and oxygen saturation.
✅ Viva Point: What suction pressure is used for adults? Standard wall pressure is **80–120 mmHg** (max 150 mmHg).
Station 03 & 09

3. Assisting in Lumbar Puncture (LP)

📌 Definition

  • Needle insertion into the L3–L4 or L4–L5 subarachnoid space to access CSF.

🎯 Purpose

  • Measure CSF opening pressure and collect diagnostic CSF samples.

⚠️ Contraindications

  • Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP) due to risk of brain herniation.

🛠️ Equipment

  • LP tray, spinal needle with stylet, manometer, 3–4 numbered tubes, Lidocaine.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Position patient in lateral fetal position (chin to chest, knees to abdomen).
  2. Maintain sterile field and support patient position strictly during insertion.
  3. Assist provider in collecting CSF into sequentially numbered tubes (1, 2, 3).
  4. Apply sterile dressing; instruct patient to lie flat for 4–8 hours post-procedure.
✅ Viva Point: Why lie flat post-LP? It prevents spinal headache caused by CSF leakage from the dural puncture site.
Station 04

4. Assisting in Thoracentesis

📌 Definition

  • Surgical perforation of the chest wall and pleural space with a needle.

🎯 Purpose

  • Aspirate pleural fluid for diagnostic analysis or to relieve dyspnea.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Do not remove >1,000 to 1,500 mL at once to avoid pulmonary edema.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Thoracentesis kit, large-bore needle/catheter, drainage bottle, local anesthetic.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Position patient upright, leaning forward over an overbed table.
  2. Monitor vital signs and instruct patient strictly not to cough or move.
  3. Assist provider with fluid aspiration into vacuum bottles.
  4. Apply occlusive sterile dressing; order post-procedure chest X-ray.
✅ Viva Point: Always watch for pneumothorax signs (sudden dyspnea, tachypnea, diminished breath sounds on affected side).
Station 05

5. Assisting in Paracentesis

📌 Definition

  • Needle insertion through abdominal wall to drain peritoneal fluid (ascites).

🎯 Purpose

  • Relieve abdominal pressure and analyze ascitic fluid.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Patient MUST void before the procedure to avoid bladder puncture!

🛠️ Equipment

  • Paracentesis kit, trocar, IV tubing, collection bottles, Lidocaine.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Have patient void. Measure weight, BP, and abdominal girth baseline.
  2. Position patient in high-Fowler’s position in bed or chair.
  3. Assist physician with sterile insertion into lower abdomen.
  4. Monitor BP continuously for hypovolemic shock; measure total drained volume.
✅ Viva Point: IV Albumin is often infused during large-volume paracentesis to prevent severe intravascular fluid shifts and hypotension.
Station 06

6. Assisting in Chest Tube Insertion

📌 Definition

  • Inserting a thoracic catheter into the pleural space attached to a water seal.

🎯 Purpose

  • Drain air (pneumothorax) or fluid (hemothorax) to re-expand the lung.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Keep drainage system upright and below chest level at all times.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Chest tube tray, drainage system (Atrium/Pleur-evac), sterile water, petroleum gauze.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Fill water seal chamber with sterile water up to the 2 cm line.
  2. Position patient arm raised over head on affected side.
  3. Connect tube immediately to drainage unit upon insertion.
  4. Secure dressing with airtight petroleum gauze; verify tidaling in water seal.
✅ Viva Point: Continuous vigorous bubbling in the water seal chamber indicates an **air leak** in the system or patient's lung!
Station 07

7. Assisting in C.T. Scan

📌 Definition

  • Computerized Tomography creating cross-sectional X-ray images of body tissues.

🎯 Purpose

  • Detect tumors, infarctions, bone fractures, and internal bleeding.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Check iodine/shellfish allergy and renal function (BUN/Creatinine) for contrast CT.

🛠️ Equipment

  • IV catheter (18–20G for contrast), contrast media, lead apron shields.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Verify NPO status if contrast is ordered; screen for pregnancy and metal objects.
  2. Establish patent IV line; explain flushing/warm sensation during dye injection.
  3. Position patient lying flat on scanner bed and instruct to lie completely still.
  4. Post-contrast: Encourage fluids to flush contrast dye from kidneys.
✅ Viva Point: Metformin must be held for 48 hours post-contrast CT to prevent acute renal failure and lactic acidosis.
Station 08

8. Cerebral Angiography

📌 Definition

  • X-ray visualization of cerebral blood vessels via femoral artery catheterization.

🎯 Purpose

  • Detect aneurysms, vascular malformations (AVM), or occlusions.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Risk of puncture site hematoma or embolic stroke during catheterization.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Angiography tray, radiopaque contrast, fluoroscopy unit, pressure dressing.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Keep NPO 6–8 hours prior. Record baseline peripheral pulses and neuro checks.
  2. Catheter inserted into femoral artery and advanced to carotid artery.
  3. Post-procedure: Apply firm pressure over femoral site for 15–30 minutes.
  4. Maintain leg straight and bed rest flat for 6–12 hours. Check pedal pulses hourly.
✅ Viva Point: Always assess **Pedal Pulses** (Dorsalis Pedis) and circulation in the affected leg post-angiogram.
Station 10

10. Assisting in Myelogram

📌 Definition

  • Radiographic study of the spinal cord following subarachnoid contrast injection.

🎯 Purpose

  • Identify herniated discs, spinal cord tumors, or nerve root compression.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Elevate head of bed 30–45° post-op if water-soluble dye was used to prevent seizures.

🛠️ Equipment

  • LP equipment, water-soluble intrathecal contrast dye, fluoroscopy table.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Assist provider in performing lumbar puncture and injecting contrast media.
  2. Tilt table during fluoroscopy to distribute dye along spinal column.
  3. Post-procedure: Keep HOB elevated 30–45 degrees for 8 hours; encourage oral fluids.
✅ Viva Point: Always check if Phenothiazines (antipsychotics) were held—they lower seizure threshold when combined with intrathecal dye.
Station 11

11. Audiometric Testing

📌 Definition

  • Non-invasive measurement of hearing acuity across sound frequencies.

🎯 Purpose

  • Differentiate conductive vs. sensorineural hearing loss.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Ensure test room is soundproofed to avoid environmental noise errors.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Audiometer, calibrated headphones, signal button, sound-treated booth.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Place headphones over patient's ears (Red = Right ear, Blue = Left ear).
  2. Instruct patient to raise hand or press button whenever tone is heard.
  3. Deliver pure tones at varying decibels and frequencies; record audiogram.
✅ Viva Point: Red headphone cushion always goes on the **Right** ear (R = Red = Right).
Station 12

12. Thyroid Scanning

📌 Definition

  • Nuclear medicine scan following administration of Radioactive Iodine (I-123 or Tc-99m).

🎯 Purpose

  • Evaluate thyroid nodules ("hot" vs "cold") and diagnose Graves' disease.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Strictly contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding!

🛠️ Equipment

  • Radioactive isotope (oral or IV), gamma scintillation camera.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Stop thyroid medications and iodine-rich foods 1–2 weeks prior.
  2. Administer radiotracer; wait 24 hours for uptake measurement.
  3. Position patient supine with neck extended under gamma camera.
✅ Viva Point: **"Cold" nodules** absorb little isotope and carry a higher risk of thyroid malignancy than "hot" nodules.
Station 13

13. Assisting in X-Ray Procedures

📌 Definition

  • Electromagnetic radiation passing through tissue to capture skeletal/organ shadows.

🎯 Purpose

  • Diagnose bone fractures, pneumonia, heart enlargement, or joint pathology.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Verify pregnancy status (LMP); apply lead aprons over reproductive organs.

🛠️ Equipment

  • X-ray machine, lead aprons/thyroid shields, image cassettes/digital detectors.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Remove all metallic objects (jewelry, zippers, snaps) from target body area.
  2. Position patient correctly (PA, AP, or Lateral views).
  3. Instruct patient to take a deep breath and hold still during exposure.
✅ Viva Point: Always enforce the **ALARA Principle** (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) for radiation exposure safety.
Station 14

14. Application of Skin Traction

📌 Definition

  • Pulling force applied directly to skin/soft tissues using adhesive or foam boots.

🎯 Purpose

  • Relieve muscle spasms and realign hip/femur fractures (Buck's Traction).

⚠️ Precautions

  • Weight Limit: Max weight for skin traction is 5–10 lbs (2.3–4.5 kg).

🛠️ Equipment

  • Buck's traction boot, pulley frame, rope, hanging weights.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Assess baseline skin integrity and neurovascular status (5 Ps).
  2. Wrap leg smoothly in traction boot; attach rope over overhead pulley.
  3. Gently lower prescribed weights; ensure weights **hang freely** off the floor.
✅ Viva Point: NEVER remove traction weights without a specific physician order!
Station 15

15. Application of Plaster Cast

📌 Definition

  • Application of rigid plaster/fiberglass bandages for fracture immobilization.

🎯 Purpose

  • Hold reduced fractures in anatomical alignment during healing.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Never use fingertips when handling wet plaster—use palms only!

🛠️ Equipment

  • Stockinette padding, plaster/fiberglass rolls, basin of warm water.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Apply tubular stockinette smoothly over limb; add soft cotton padding.
  2. Submerge plaster rolls in warm water until bubbles cease; squeeze gently.
  3. Wrap plaster spirally; mold smoothly using **palms of hands**.
  4. Leave cast exposed to air until dry; monitor 5 Ps for Compartment Syndrome.
✅ Viva Point: What are the 5 Ps of Compartment Syndrome? **Pain, Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis**.
Station 16

16. Application of Eye Bandage

📌 Definition

  • Application of a protective eye patch or Fox metal shield over the orbit.

🎯 Purpose

  • Protect eye from light, dust, or trauma post-surgery or injury.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Never apply direct pressure onto an injured eyeball!

🛠️ Equipment

  • Sterile eye pad, metal shield, paper tape, antiseptic wipes.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Have patient close both eyes gently.
  2. Place sterile eye pad over closed eyelid.
  3. Apply tape diagonally from cheek to middle of forehead.
✅ Viva Point: In traumatic eye laceration, use a rigid eye shield—never press down with a soft pad.
Station 17

17. Eye Irrigation

📌 Definition

  • Flushing the conjunctival sac with fluid solution.

🎯 Purpose

  • Flush chemical irritants or foreign bodies from the eye.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Always flush from Inner Canthus to Outer Canthus!

🛠️ Equipment

  • Sterile Normal Saline (0.9%), IV tubing/Morgan lens, emesis basin.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Tilt patient's head toward the affected eye side.
  2. Hold eyelids open; direct fluid stream gently from inner to outer canthus.
  3. Catch fluid runoff with an emesis basin held against the cheek.
✅ Viva Point: Why flush inner to outer? To prevent contaminating the lacrimal duct and the uninjured opposite eye!
Station 18

18. Ear Irrigation

📌 Definition

  • Flushing external auditory canal with warm water or saline.

🎯 Purpose

  • Remove impacted cerumen (wax) or non-vegetable foreign bodies.

⚠️ Contraindications

  • Perforated tympanic membrane or vegetable foreign bodies (e.g. beans swell!).

🛠️ Equipment

  • Ear syringe/Waterpik, warm water (~37°C), otoscope, basin.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Inspect ear canal with otoscope to verify intact eardrum.
  2. For Adults: Pull pinna UP and BACK (Children: Down and Back).
  3. Direct fluid flow toward roof of ear canal—never directly at eardrum!
✅ Viva Point: Always use body-temperature solution (~37°C). Cold or hot water causes severe vestibular stimulation and vertigo!
Station 19

19. Blood Sugar Monitoring (BSR)

📌 Definition

  • Capillary blood glucose measurement via fingerstick lancet sample.

🎯 Purpose

  • Screen and monitor glucose levels to manage diabetes or hypoglycemia.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Wipe away first drop of blood to avoid alcohol/tissue fluid dilution.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Glucometer, test strips, sterile lancet, alcohol swab, cotton ball.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Clean lateral side of fingertip with alcohol and allow to dry completely.
  2. Prick side of fingertip with lancet; wipe away the first drop of blood.
  3. Apply second drop to test strip; record glucometer reading.
✅ Normal Values: Fasting BSR = 70–99 mg/dL | Postprandial (2hr) = <140 mg/dL.
Station 20

20. Cardiac Monitoring / Telemetry

📌 Definition

  • Continuous transmission of cardiac electrical activity via electrode leads.

🎯 Purpose

  • Detect lethal cardiac arrhythmias (VTach, VFib, Asystole) continuously.

⚠️ Precautions

  • Ensure clean skin contact; change electrode pads every 24–48 hours.

🛠️ Equipment

  • Telemetry transmitter pack, 3-lead or 5-lead cable set, pre-gelled electrodes.

📋 Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Prep chest skin (clip hair, clean oil with alcohol swab).
  2. Apply 5 leads: White (Right Shoulder), Black (Left Shoulder), Brown (Chest Center), Green (Right Lower Abdomen), Red (Left Lower Abdomen).
  3. Verify clear ECG rhythm baseline on telemetry display monitor.
✅ Viva Memory Trick: "White on right, snow over grass (White top right, Green bottom right). Smoke over fire (Black top left, Red bottom left). Chocolate close to the heart (Brown center)."

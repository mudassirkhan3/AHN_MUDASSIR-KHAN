🦴 Unit I: Orthopedic Nursing

Soft Tissue Injury: Damage to muscles, ligaments, and tendons. Managed using the RICE protocol (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation).

Osteomyelitis: Severe infection of the bone bone marrow, most commonly caused by Staphylococcus aureus.

Rheumatoid Arthritis vs Osteoarthritis: RA is a systemic autoimmune inflammation of the synovial membrane; OA is a non-inflammatory wear-and-tear of articular cartilage.

Paget's Disease (Osteitis Deformans): Disorder of localized bone remodeling characterized by excessive bone resorption followed by disorganized, weak bone formation.

Compartment Syndrome: Elevated pressure within a myofascial compartment compromising circulation. Signified by the 6 Ps: Pain, Paresthesia, Pallor, Paralysis, Pulselessness, Poikilothermia.

🩸 Unit II: Endocrine Nursing

Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA): Life-threatening complication of Type 1 DM characterized by Hyperglycemia (>250 mg/dL), Metabolic Acidosis (pH <7.30), and Ketonuria. Hallmark sign: Kussmaul breathing (deep, rapid breathing with fruity odor).

Hypocalcemia Signs (Post-Thyroidectomy):
• Trousseau's Sign: Carpopedal spasm induced by inflating a BP cuff above systolic pressure.
• Chvostek's Sign: Twitching of facial muscles in response to tapping over the facial nerve.

Addisonian Crisis: Acute adrenal insufficiency caused by deficiency of cortisol/aldosterone. Characterized by severe hypotension, hyponatremia, and hyperkalemia.

Dawn Phenomenon vs Somogyi Effect: Dawn = Early morning hyperglycemia due to natural cortisol release; Somogyi = Rebound morning hyperglycemia triggered by nocturnal (2 AM) hypoglycemia.

🧠 Unit III: Neurological Nursing

Normal ICP & Cushing's Triad: Normal intracranial pressure is 5 to 15 mmHg. Increased ICP leads to Cushing's Triad: 1. Systolic HTN with widened pulse pressure, 2. Bradycardia, 3. Bradypnea/Irregular respirations.

Meningitis Diagnostic Signs:
• Kernig's Sign: Resistance or pain when extending the knee with the hip flexed at 90°.
• Brudzinski's Sign: Involuntary flexion of hips and knees when the neck is passively flexed.

Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS): Autoimmune destruction of myelin sheaths on peripheral nerves leading to ascending symmetrical motor weakness/paralysis.

Myasthenia Gravis (MG): Autoimmune disorder destroying acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction causing fatigable muscle weakness. Diagnosed with the Tensilon (Edrophonium) test.

🫀 Unit IV: Cardiovascular Disorders

Myocardial Infarction (MI): Irreversible necrosis of heart muscle secondary to prolonged ischemia. Gold-standard diagnostic biomarkers: Troponin I & T (elevate within 3-4 hours).

Virchow's Triad (DVT Risk): 1. Venous stasis, 2. Endothelial damage, 3. Hypercoagulability.

Infective Endocarditis Stigmata: Osler's nodes (painful nodules on fingers/toes), Janeway lesions (painless flat erythematous macules on palms/soles), and Roth's spots (retinal hemorrhages).

MONA Emergency Protocol: Morphine, Oxygen, Nitroglycerin, Aspirin.

🫁 Unit V: Pulmonary Nursing

Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS): Non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema characterized by severe dyspnea and Refractory Hypoxemia (low PaO2 despite 100% O2 administration; PaO2/FiO2 ratio ≤ 300 mmHg).

COPD Components:
• Chronic Bronchitis ("Blue Bloater"): Productive cough for ≥3 months in 2 consecutive years.
• Emphysema ("Pink Puffer"): Destruction of alveolar walls leading to air trapping and barrel chest.

Pneumonia Classic Physical Finding: Increased tactile fremitus, dullness to percussion over consolidation, and rust-colored sputum (Streptococcus pneumoniae).

👁️ Unit VI: Eye & ENT Nursing

Meniere's Disease Triad: 1. Episodic Vertigo, 2. Sensorineural Hearing Loss, 3. Tinnitus (along with a feeling of fullness in the inner ear).

Cataract vs Retinal Detachment:
• Cataract: Painless, gradual clouding of the crystalline lens.
• Retinal Detachment: Sudden, painless vision loss described as a "dark shadow or curtain coming down" over the visual field with light flashes (photopsia) and floaters.

🔥 Unit VII: Burns Management

Parkland Formula (Fluid Resuscitation):

Total Lactated Ringer's (24 Hours) = 4 mL × Weight (kg) × % TBSA Burned

Administration Rule: Administer 50% of total volume in the first 8 hours (calculated from time of injury), and the remaining 50% over the next 16 hours.

Rule of Nines (Adults): Head & Neck = 9%, Each Arm = 9%, Anterior Trunk = 18%, Posterior Trunk = 18%, Each Leg = 18%, Perineum = 1%.

Burn Depth Classification: 1st Degree (Superficial - painful, dry), 2nd Degree (Partial Thickness - blisters, extremely painful), 3rd Degree (Full Thickness - leathery eschar, painless due to destroyed nerve endings).

Adult Health Nursing II (AHN-II) High-Yield Master Guide

Standardized clinical definitions, key diagnostic signs, examiner favorites, nursing priorities, and management pearls structured directly from the academic syllabus.

Unit I: Orthopedic Nursing

8 Hours • 1 Credit

1. Soft Tissue Injury (Sprains & Strains)

RICE Protocol

Sprain: Injury to a ligament (bone-to-bone joint stabilizer) caused by wrenching or twisting.
Strain: Injury to a muscle or tendon (muscle-to-bone attachment) caused by overuse or excessive stretching.

⚡ Management: Use RICE (Rest, Ice for 20 mins, Compression, Elevation above heart).

2. Fractures, Compartment Syndrome & Amputation

🔥 Examiner Favorite

Fracture: A disruption or break in the continuity of the bone structure.
Compartment Syndrome: Increased pressure within a non-yielding fascia compartment compromising blood flow.

⚡ The 6 Ps of Compartment Syndrome:

Pain (out of proportion), Paresthesia, Pallor, Paralysis, Pulselessness, Poikilothermia.

🚨 Viva Alert: DO NOT elevate limb or apply ice in compartment syndrome. Treatment is emergency Fasciotomy.

3. Paget’s Disease & Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)

Paget's Disease (Osteitis Deformans): A chronic bone disorder marked by excessive, disorganized bone turnover leading to structurally weak, enlarged bones.
Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA): A systemic, chronic autoimmune inflammatory disorder affecting the synovial membrane of joints symmetrically.

🔑 RA Hallmarks: Morning stiffness lasting >1 hour, symmetric joint involvement, ulnar drift, and swan-neck deformities.

4. Osteomyelitis, Osteoarthritis & Osteoporosis

Osteomyelitis: Severe pyogenic infection of the bone, bone marrow, and surrounding tissue (most common organism: Staphylococcus aureus).
Osteoarthritis (OA): Non-inflammatory degenerative joint disease caused by progressive loss of articular cartilage (asymmetric, age-related).
Osteoporosis: Metabolic bone disease characterized by low bone mineral density ($T\text{-score} \le -2.5$) and structural deterioration.

5. Bone Tumors & Bone Tuberculosis (Pott’s Disease)

Osteosarcoma: The most common primary malignant bone tumor, predominantly affecting the metaphysis of long bones in young adults.
Pott’s Disease (Tuberculous Spondylitis): Extrapulmonary tuberculosis affecting the spine, leading to kyphosis (gibbus deformity), vertebral collapse, and potential spinal cord compression.

Unit II: Endocrine Nursing

12 Hours • 2 Credits

1. Parathyroid Disorders & Tetany Assessment

Hypoparathyroidism: Inadequate Parathyroid Hormone (PTH) leading to hypocalcemia ($<8.5\text{ mg/dL}$) and neuromuscular excitability (Tetany).

⚡ Viva High-Yield Clinical Signs:

1. Trousseau's Sign: Carpopedal spasm induced by inflating BP cuff above systolic pressure for 3 mins.

2. Chvostek's Sign: Facial twitching elicited by tapping over the facial nerve anterior to the ear.

2 & 3. Diabetes Mellitus, Hypoglycemia & DKA

🔥 Guaranteed Question

Diabetes Mellitus: Metabolic disorder of chronic hyperglycemia due to defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both.
DKA Triad: Hyperglycemia ($>250\text{ mg/dL}$), Metabolic Acidosis ($\text{pH} <7.30$, $\text{HCO}_3 <18$), and Ketonemia/Ketonuria.

💡 DKA Signs & Nursing Priority:

• Signs: Kussmaul respirations (deep, rapid air-hungering), fruity acetone breath.

• Priority: Rehydration with 0.9% Normal Saline before IV Regular Insulin drip.

4. Thyroid Disorders (Hyper vs. Hypo)

Grave’s Disease (Hyperthyroidism): Autoimmune overproduction of T3/T4 resulting in hypermetabolism, heat intolerance, weight loss, tachycardia, and exophthalmos.
Myxedema (Hypothyroidism): Deficiency of T3/T4 causing hypometabolism, cold intolerance, weight gain, bradycardia, and non-pitting edema.

🚨 Thyroid Storm Crisis: Acute severe hyperthyroidism presenting with extreme hyperthermia ($>103^\circ\text{F}$), severe tachycardia, and delirium.

5. Adrenal Gland Disorders

Cushing’s Syndrome: Excessive glucocorticoids (cortisol). Features: Moon face, buffalo hump, central obesity, purple striae, hyperglycemia.
Addison’s Disease: Adrenocortical insufficiency (low cortisol/aldosterone). Features: Hyperpigmentation, hypotension, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia.

⚡ Pheochromocytoma: Catecholamine-secreting adrenal tumor presenting with the triad: Headache, Diaphoresis, and Severe Paroxysmal Hypertension.

Unit III: Neurological Nursing

12 Hours • 3 Credits

1. Intracranial Pressure (ICP) & Head Injury

🚨 Emergency Core

Normal ICP is 5 to 15 mmHg. Sustained ICP >20 mmHg is pathologic.

⚠️ Cushing's Triad (Late Sign of Brain Herniation):

1. Systolic Hypertension with Widened Pulse Pressure

2. Bradycardia (Slow, Bounding Heart Rate)

3. Irregular/Bradypneic Respirations

2. Spinal Cord Injury (SCI) & Stroke (CVA)

Autonomic Dysreflexia: Life-threatening emergency in SCI at/above T6 triggered by noxious stimuli (e.g., distended bladder). Features severe hypertension and bradycardia.
CVA (Stroke): Sudden focal neurological deficit caused by Ischemic (87%) or Hemorrhagic vascular occlusion/rupture.

⚡ Ischemic Stroke Thrombolytic Window: IV tPA (Alteplase) administered within 3 to 4.5 hours of symptom onset.

3 & 4. Meningitis & Encephalitis

Meningitis: Acute inflammation of the meninges surrounding the brain and spinal cord.

🩺 Diagnostic Meningeal Signs:

• Kernig's Sign: Inability to fully extend the knee when thigh is flexed at 90 degrees due to pain.

• Brudzinski's Sign: Involuntary flexion of the hips/knees when the neck is passively flexed.

5 & 6. Seizures & Myasthenia Gravis (MG)

Status Epilepticus: Seizure activity lasting >5 minutes or recurrent seizures without full recovery of consciousness (Emergency drug: IV Lorazepam/Diazepam).
Myasthenia Gravis: Autoimmune attack on acetylcholine receptors at neuromuscular junction causing fluctuating muscle weakness and ptosis.

7, 8 & 9. Guillain-Barré (GBS) & Trigeminal Neuralgia

GBS: Acute post-infectious autoimmune demyelination causing ascending, symmetrical motor weakness.
Trigeminal Neuralgia (Tic Douloureux): Severe, sharp, shooting facial pain along CN V branches.

🚨 GBS Danger: Respiratory paralysis when weakness reaches the diaphragm (phrenic nerve). Monitor Forced Vital Capacity!

10, 11 & 12. Parkinson’s, Alzheimer’s & Glasgow Coma Scale

Parkinson’s Disease: Loss of dopamine-producing neurons in substantia nigra. Classic Tetrad: Resting tremor (pill-rolling), Rigidity (cogwheel), Akinesia/Bradykinesia, Postural instability.
Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS): Evaluates Eye (4), Verbal (5), Motor (8) responses. Range: 3 to 15. Score $\le 8$ indicates coma ("Coma at 8, intubate").

Unit IV: Cardiovascular Disorders

10 Hours • 2 Credits

1 & 2. Aneurysm, DVT & Virchow's Triad

Aneurysm: Localized permanent dilation of an arterial wall (>50% increase in diameter).
Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT): Thrombus formation in deep veins, usually lower extremities.

🩸 Virchow's Triad (DVT Risk): 1. Venous Stasis, 2. Endothelial Injury, 3. Hypercoagulability.

3. Hypertension & Hypertensive Crisis

Persistent elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure ($\ge 130/80\text{ mmHg}$).
Hypertensive Emergency: BP >180/120 mmHg WITH acute target organ damage (e.g., encephalopathy, acute MI).

4, 5 & 6. Pericarditis, Myocarditis & Endocarditis

Pericarditis: Inflammation of pericardium presenting with chest pain relieved by sitting forward and a Pericardial Friction Rub.
Infective Endocarditis: Infection of endocardium/valves. Features: Janeway lesions (painless macular spots), Osler's nodes (painful nodules), Roth spots.

7 & 8. Myocardial Infarction (MI) & Cardiac Markers

🔥 High Yield

MI: Irreversible myocardial necrosis caused by severe ischemia.

🧪 Cardiac Biomarkers:

• Troponin I & T: Gold standard; specific, rises in 3–4 hrs, stays elevated 7–14 days.

• MONA Protocol: Morphine, Oxygen, Nitroglycerin, Aspirin.

Unit V: Pulmonary Nursing

10 Hours • 2 Credits

1–5. Upper Respiratory Infections & Lung Abscess

Sinusitis/Pharyngitis/Tonsillitis: Inflammation of paranasal sinuses, pharynx, or palatine tonsils.
Lung Abscess: Necrotizing lung infection characterized by a localized pus-filled cavity, often caused by aspiration of oral secretions.

6 & 7. Pneumonia & COPD

Pneumonia: Acute infection of lung parenchyma causing alveolar consolidation (CAP vs. HAP).
COPD: Progressive airflow limitation combining Chronic Bronchitis ("Blue Bloaters", productive cough $\ge 3$ mos for 2 consecutive yrs) and Emphysema ("Pink Puffers", alveolar destruction).

8–11. Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) & Chest Trauma

ARDS: Non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema secondary to alveolar-capillary membrane damage.
Flail Chest: Fracture of 3 or more contiguous ribs in 2 or more places causing paradoxical chest movement (sucking in during inspiration, bulging during expiration).

💡 ARDS Hallmark: Refractory Hypoxemia — arterial hypoxemia that does NOT improve even with 100% supplemental oxygen administration.

Unit VI: Eye & ENT Nursing

4 Hours • 1 Credit

1 & 2. Mastoiditis & Meniere's Disease

Mastoiditis: Infection of mastoid air cells, usually a complication of untreated acute otitis media.
Meniere's Disease: Inner ear disorder due to endolymphatic hydrops.

👂 Meniere's Triad: 1. Episodic Vertigo, 2. Sensorineural Hearing Loss, 3. Tinnitus & Aural Fullness.

3, 4 & 5. Cataract & Retinal Detachment

Cataract: Opacification/clouding of the natural crystalline lens causing painless, progressive blurred vision and glare.
Retinal Detachment: Separation of neurosensory retina from retinal pigment epithelium.

👁️ Retinal Detachment Sign: Painless vision loss described as a "dark curtain being pulled over the visual field", preceded by light flashes and floaters.

Unit VII: Burns & Fluid Resuscitation

Core Unit

1. Definition & Classification of Burns

Burn Injury: Tissue damage resulting from thermal, chemical, electrical, or radiation energy.

1st Degree (Superficial):

Epidermis only; red, painful, dry, no blisters (e.g., sunburn).

2nd Degree (Partial Thickness):

Epidermis + Dermis; fluid-filled blisters, extremely painful, weeping.

3rd Degree (Full Thickness):

Destroys all skin layers + subcutaneous; leathery, eschar, painless (nerve destruction).

📐 Parkland Fluid Resuscitation Formula:
Total 24h Fluid (mL) = 4 mL × Body Weight (kg) × % TBSA Burned
⏱️ First 8 Hours:

Give 50% of total volume (calculated from time of injury).

⏱️ Next 16 Hours:

Give remaining 50% of total volume.

Fluid of choice: Lactated Ringer's (LR) solution. Best indicator of adequate resuscitation: Urine output $0.5\text{ to }1.0\text{ mL/kg/hr}$.

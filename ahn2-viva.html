Unit I • Orthopedics & Trauma 🔥 Top Examiner Favorite

1. Compartment Syndrome & Fasciotomy

Definition: A life-threatening extremity condition where increased tissue pressure within a non-yielding osteofascial compartment compromises capillary perfusion, leading to muscle tissue hypoxia and tissue necrosis.

⚡ The 6 Ps (Must Recite in Viva):
  • 1. Pain: Severe, deep, out of proportion to injury, unrelieved by opioids.
  • 2. Paresthesia: Pins and needles (earliest sensory deficit).
  • 3. Pallor: Pale skin tone due to ischemia.
  • 4. Paralysis: Inability to move digits (late sign).
  • 5. Pulselessness: Absent distal pulse (very late sign).
  • 6. Poikilothermia: Affected limb feels cool/cold.
🩺 Management & RICE Protocol:

Soft Tissue Injury (Sprains/Strains): Use RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation).

🚨 CRITICAL VIVA RULE: If acute Compartment Syndrome is suspected, DO NOT elevate the limb (decreases arterial blood flow) and DO NOT apply ice (causes vasoconstriction). Prepare patient for emergency Fasciotomy.
Unit II • Endocrine Disorders 🔥 High-Yield

2. Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) vs. Tetany Assessment

DKA Definition: An acute, life-threatening complication of Diabetes Mellitus (primarily Type 1) characterized by the triad of Hyperglycemia (>250 mg/dL), Metabolic Acidosis (pH <7.30, serum bicarbonate <18 mEq/L), and Ketonemia/Ketonuria due to insulin lack.

🫁 DKA Clinical Features & Breathing:
  • • Kussmaul Respirations: Deep, rapid, air-hungering breathing pattern to blow off CO2 and compensate for metabolic acidosis.
  • • Fruity/Acetone Breath: Due to volatile ketone excretion via lungs.
  • • Initial Nursing Priority: Rehydration with Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl) before starting Regular Insulin drip.
🦴 Hypocalcemia Assessment Signs:

1. Trousseau's Sign: Carpopedal spasm (flexion of wrist and metacarpophalangeal joints) induced by inflating BP cuff 20 mmHg above systolic BP for 3 minutes.

2. Chvostek's Sign: Twitching of facial muscles elicited by tapping the facial nerve anterior to the ear lobe.

Unit III • Neurological Nursing 🔥 Top Examiner Favorite

3. Increased ICP, Cushing's Triad & Guillain-Barré Syndrome

Intracranial Pressure (ICP): Normal range is 5 to 15 mmHg. Sustained pressure >20 mmHg requires prompt intervention to prevent brain herniation.

⚠️ Cushing's Triad (Late Sign of Brain Herniation):

Contrast this with shock (where HR goes up and BP drops). Cushing's Triad presents with:

1. Systolic Hypertension (Widened Pulse Pressure)
2. Bradycardia (Slow, Bounding Heart Rate)
3. Bradypnea / Irregular Breathing
🧠 Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS) Core Key:

An acute post-infectious autoimmune polyneuropathy characterized by progressive, ascending symmetrical motor weakness and loss of deep tendon reflexes.
Viva Question: What is the main cause of death? Respiratory failure when weakness ascends to the diaphragm (phrenic nerve involvement). Monitor Vital Capacity closely!

Unit IV • Cardiovascular Nursing

4. Myocardial Infarction Biomarkers & Virchow's Triad

Myocardial Infarction (MI): Irreversible necrosis of cardiac muscle caused by prolonged severe ischemia.

🧪 Cardiac Biomarkers:
  • • Troponin I & T: Most specific and sensitive cardiac gold standard. Rises in 3–4 hrs, stays elevated for 7–14 days.
  • • CK-MB: Useful for detecting re-infarction (returns to baseline in 48–72 hours).
🩸 Virchow's Triad (DVT Pathogenesis):

1. Venous Stasis (immobility, heart failure)
2. Endothelial Injury (trauma, surgery, IV lines)
3. Hypercoagulability (malignancy, dehydration, estrogen)

Unit V • Pulmonary Nursing

5. ARDS & Refractory Hypoxemia

ARDS Definition: A form of acute, diffuse inflammatory lung injury that leads to increased pulmonary vascular permeability, decreased lung compliance, and widespread alveolar collapse.

💡 Hallmark Viva Concept: Refractory Hypoxemia

Refractory Hypoxemia is severe arterial hypoxemia ($PaO_2 / FiO_2 \le 300\text{ mmHg}$) that fails to improve even when high concentrations of supplemental 100% oxygen are delivered due to intrapulmonary right-to-left shunting.

Unit VI • ENT & Ophthalmic Nursing

6. Meniere's Disease vs. Retinal Detachment

👂 Meniere's Triad (Inner Ear):
  • 1. Episodic Vertigo (Severe spinning sensation)
  • 2. Fluctuating Sensorineural Hearing Loss
  • 3. Low-pitched Tinnitus & Aural Fullness
👁️ Retinal Detachment Sign:

Separation of neurosensory retina from pigment epithelium. Classic symptom: Painless visual field loss described as a "dark shade or curtain being pulled over vision" accompanied by sudden photopsia (flashes of light) and floaters.

Unit VII • Burns & Fluid Resuscitation 🔥 Guaranteed Question

7. Parkland Formula & Wallace Rule of Nines

Used for emergency fluid resuscitation during the first 24 hours post-burn in patients with >20% TBSA burns. Fluid of choice: Lactated Ringer's (LR) solution.

📐 Parkland Formula Equation:
Total 24h Fluid (mL) = 4 mL × Body Weight (kg) × % TBSA Burned
⏱️ First 8 Hours:

Administer 50% (half) of total calculated 24-hour volume (calculated from time of injury, NOT hospital admission).

⏱️ Remaining 16 Hours:

Administer remaining 50% of total volume across the next 16 hours.
